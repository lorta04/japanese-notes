11. ～に関係なく・無視して
11.1 ～を問わず　ー　～がどうかは問題ではなく、どれにも同じことが言える　
ー　「～を問とわず」 = "regardless of ~~" in the sense of "no questions asked about ~~" even though one could ask questions about "~~" if one wanted to.
ー　Examples: 「年齢ねんれいを問わず (age)」、「性別せいべつを問わず (gender)」、「経験けいけんを問わず (experience)」, etc.
ー　いろいろな違いや幅のある言葉（年齢・国籍・天候）や対立する言葉（男女・内外・有無）
11.2 ～にかかわりなく・～にかかわらず　ー　～には関係なく同じようになる
ー　「～に関かかわらず」 = "regardless of ~~" in the sense of "~~ bearing no influence (on another event)" mostly because one has no control of "~~"
ー　Examples: 「サッカーの試合しあいは、天候てんこうに関わらず行おこなわれる。」= "Soccer matches are held regardless of the weather."
ー　幅のある言葉（距離・金額・大きさ）。また、対立する言葉（行く、行かない・多い、少ない）や「（疑問詞）～か」
ー　名
11.3 ～もかまわず　ー　普通なら気にする～のことを気にしないで行動する　ー　人目もかまわず、道で大泣きしている人を見かけた　ー　名・普通形（な形な/である・名な/である）＋の
11.4 ～はともかく（として）　ー　anyhow; anyway; regardless; in any case; whether or not ~
ー　N＋はともかく、～ means that N might be true, but it doesn't really matter compared to ~. Take for example:
ー　https://japanese.stackexchange.com/questions/58671/difference-between-%E3%81%AF%E3%81%A8%E3%82%82%E3%81%8B%E3%81%8F%E3%81%A8%E3%81%97%E3%81%A6-and-%E3%81%AF%E3%81%95%E3%81%A6%E3%81%8A%E3%81%8D

ー　名
11.5 ～はさておき　ー　setting aside ~
ー　N＋はさておき、～ means more "setting aside N, let's look at ~" (though ~ might still be important, it just isn't as big a priority). For example:
ー　どんな家がいいかはさておき、どんな地域に引っ越したいかを考えよ。　ー　Leaving aside what kind of house would be good, think about what kind of area you want to move to.
ー　This implies that what kind of house is good DOES matter (unlike in はともかく), and after you decided something else we might return to this question, but the priority first is what area you want to move to.
ー　名

14. ～けれど
14.1 ～にもかかわらず　ー　“although/despite X…” This can be used to indicate that an action/state Y is done/happens despite “X.” Or, the speaker is not constrained by “X” and Y, thus, can take place.
ー　The word "～にもかかわらず" expresses a situation that differs from the prediction from the previous sentence in the second sentence. There is no blame or criticism involved. Usually it is simply an objective way of indicating an adversarial relationship, but sometimes the speaker's feelings can be included with the focus particle "～も" .
ー　名・普通系（な形である・名である）
14.2 ～ものの・～とはいうものの　ー　～事実なのだが、その事実から当然想像されることが起こらない　ー　ものの is sort of a more "written" style of なのに
ー　The word "～ものの" is used to refer to a matter or situation that is contrary to or incompatible with the statement made in the previous sentence, while at the same time acknowledging it. In such cases, the second sentence is often used to express remorse or regret for what was said in the first sentence while acknowledging it.
ー　動・形普通形（な形な・である）＋ものの

"おそれがある" includes the meaning of something bad or something to avoid.
"かねない" does not necessarily include such a meaning.
Examples:

The children are energetic, and they could start running at any moment.

ー　普通形（な形woだ・名woだ）

ものだ (mono da):
The ものだ expression is used to indicate a general truth, something that is universally or inherently true, or a widely accepted fact. It can be translated as "it is (originally/naturally) that~" or "it is a truth that~". This expression is often used to emphasize what is considered natural, proper, or common sense in a given situation.
For example:

若いころに苦労するものだ (wakai koro ni kurou suru mono da)
"It's natural to struggle when you're young."
というものだ (to iu mono da):
The というものだ expression is used to emphasize that something is true from a common sense or conventional perspective. It can be translated as "it is indeed that~" or "it is common sense that~". This expression is used when stating something that is widely recognized or agreed upon.
For example:

勉強すれば成績が上がるというものだ (benkyou sureba seiseki ga agaru to iu mono da)
"It's common sense that if you study, your grades will improve."
In summary, both expressions convey something that is true or widely accepted. However, ものだ emphasizes the inherent or natural truth, while というものだ focuses on the common sense or conventional aspect of the statement.

In the context of the explanation you provided, "reduces sense of commitment" means that using ものだ in the sentence makes the speaker's emotion sound more like a general observation or statement, rather than a personal expression of their own feelings. By using ものだ, the speaker is distancing themselves from the emotion to some extent, making it sound less like a personal commitment to that feeling.
To recap, the three expressions differ in the following ways:
ことだ: Used to express strong emotions like surprise, admiration, or irony in response to someone else's situation. It doesn't work well for expressing one's own emotions or experiences.
ものだ: Used to express strong feelings, but turns the raw emotion into a general observation or statement, which reduces the sense of personal commitment to the feeling. It may not be suitable for expressing very personal emotions, especially in combination with certain adjectives or phrases.
ものがある: Used to partially acknowledge one's own emotions, making it more suitable for expressing personal feelings in the given example.
